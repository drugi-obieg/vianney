Research of 2026-11-26. Question from the owner: "make sure I can download the AI model (the smaller one, not Gemini Nano, the other 300 MB one)".

How to read the tags

## 0. Short answer

- **The model is Kev-0.6B**, in the browser build `onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX` (base Qwen3-0.6B-Base, Apache-2.0, English only). The files to download are **338.7 MB** at q4f16 (WebGPU with `shader-f16`) or **376.1 MB** at q4 (WebAssembly, or WebGPU without `shader-f16`); with tokenizer and config the download is **345.7 MB** or **383.1 MB**. Only one of the two sets is needed. [verified] https://huggingface.co/api/models/onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX?blobs=true
- **It can run offline after a one-time download, in principle.** The runtime (Transformers.js bundle plus the onnxruntime WASM loader and binary, about 27.5 MB, 6.8 MB zipped) must be vendored in the extension and only the weights are downloaded; Chrome's own guidance says models are not remote hosted code. Whether it really loads under the MV3 CSP and runs on WebGPU in an offscreen document on the owner's Mac is **[not verified]**: it needs a half-day spike.
- **Biggest risk: accuracy on this project's questions.** The card's out-of-domain accuracy is 0.62 (dev) and 0.642 (locked test) on a mix of public tasks; "held-out policy reasoning fails" (6 to 11 percent on programmatic policy pairs); a third party measured 55 to 57 percent zero-shot on three of its own yes/no questions (in 33 machine-translated languages, so not directly comparable). The project's rules are policy-like ("is the name used as a curse and not in prayer"). Kev-0.6B zero-shot may be near the lexicon-only baseline. A cheap Node test on the project's 185 `example:`/`spared:` lines should come before any UI work. [verified: the card numbers, see 1.6; not verified: the guess about this project's rules]
- **Other risks**: ORT's WebGPU loader contains a `new Function` call that the MV3 CSP forbids (probably dead code, [not verified]); no published memory number; no published WASM speed; the trained context is 384 state tokens, not 8,192.
- **Work estimate**: a spike of half a day to a day; the smallest working download button about one day after the spike; the full version asked for here (resumable, SHA-256 pinned, delete, status UI, tests) several days (4 to 6 working days), without any accuracy work. [not verified: my estimate, see 5.5]

### 1.3 What the ONNX graph is (read from the files)

- **Inputs and output.** Two int64 inputs `input_ids` and `attention_mask` of shape `[batch_size, sequence_length]`; one output `logits` of shape `[batch_size, sequence_length]` (float16 in the q4f16 file, float32 in the q4 file). One value per token, not per vocabulary entry. [verified: parsed `onnx/model_q4f16.onnx` and `onnx/model_q4.onnx`]
- **Opsets**: `ai.onnx` 21 and `com.microsoft` 1. About 5,900 nodes, of which 196 are `com.microsoft::MatMulNBits` (4-bit weights) and 1 is `com.microsoft::GatherBlockQuantized` (the 4-bit embedding table). The rest are small standard ops (`Constant`, `Unsqueeze`, `Shape`, `Gather`, `Mul`, `Where`, `CumSum`, `Range`, `Softmax`, `SimplifiedLayerNormalization` and so on). [verified: parsed the same files]
- **Attention is not fused.** Tracing back from the layer-0 `Softmax`: `MatMul` (query times key) then `Mul` (scale) then `Add` of a mask that the graph builds itself (the trace shows `Unsqueeze`, `Where`, `Cast`, `Not`); there are 28 `Softmax` nodes for 28 layers and no `GroupQueryAttention` or `MultiHeadAttention` node. So the score tensor is materialised, and memory grows with the square of the sequence length (section 4). [verified: traced layer 0 in the graph]
- **All weights are external**: 451 (q4f16) and 455 (q4) initializers point at one file each, `model_q4f16.onnx_data` and `model_q4.onnx_data`, and the extents end exactly at the file size. So onnxruntime needs `externalData: [{path: 'model_q4f16.onnx_data', data: ...}]`. [verified: parsed the external-data entries]
- **Largest tensors**: the int4 embedding table `[151936, 1024]` is 77,791,232 bytes plus fp16 scales of 9,723,904 bytes; every layer weight is at most 1,572,864 bytes. The WebGPU default limits are 128 MiB per storage binding and 256 MiB per buffer, so no single tensor needs raised limits. [verified: graph parse and https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/#limits]
- **No language-model head.** The graph has no vocabulary projection and no KV-cache inputs or outputs; the generic text-generation build `onnx-community/Qwen3-0.6B-ONNX` has a q4f16 file of 569.8 MB, and the missing head and the 4-bit embedding table are the likely reason for the difference. [verified: op list and sizes https://huggingface.co/api/models/onnx-community/Qwen3-0.6B-ONNX?blobs=true; not verified: the reason]
- **Architecture numbers** (`config.json`): 28 layers, hidden 1024, 16 attention heads, 8 KV heads, head dim 128, intermediate 3072, vocabulary 151,936, tied embeddings. [verified] https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX/resolve/main/config.json

### 1.4 What q4f16 and q4 are, and which is needed where

- **q4**: 4-bit `MatMulNBits` weights (block 32), embedding as `GatherBlockQuantized`, pointer head in fp32, everything else fp32. **q4f16**: the same weights, but "runs the rest of the graph in fp16 for WebGPU". [verified] https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX (the card's "Usage with Transformers.js" section)
- **open-jev picks q4f16 only on WebGPU when the adapter has `shader-f16`, and q4 everywhere else (WASM, Node, WebGPU without f16).** [verified] https://registry.npmjs.org/open-jev/-/open-jev-0.1.2.tgz (`dist/index.js`, `README.md`)
- **Whether q4f16 would run on the WASM provider is not documented.** open-jev never tries it. [not verified]
- **Why q4f16 is not the WASM choice (my reading)**: its logits and many initializers are fp16, and the CPU provider has few fp16 kernels. [not verified: inference]
- **`shader-f16` on Apple Silicon**: Chrome shipped the feature in Chrome 120 and Google measured a Llama2 7B f16 model on an Apple M1 Pro as 28 percent faster at prefill and 41 percent faster at decoding than f32; a third party ran kev-0.6b q4f16 on an M3 Pro "(shader-f16)". Whether the owner's chip exposes it is unknown; detect it at run time with `adapter.features.has('shader-f16')`. [verified for M1 Pro and M3 Pro; not verified for the owner's Mac] https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-120 and https://github.com/nico-martin/open-jev/issues/1
- **Quality loss from 4-bit quantisation of the stock model: no published numbers.** The only datum is from a fine-tune by a third party: "The q4f16 graph agrees with the q4 one to 0.001 on the questions above" (q4 against q4f16, not against fp32). The full-precision ONNX export is not published. [verified: statement exists] https://huggingface.co/jean-is/kev-0.6b-reflexes-ONNX and https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX

### 2.2 API for "a question, a text, a probability"

```js
import { OpenJev, noul, score } from "open-jev";
const jev = await OpenJev.load({ model: "kev-0.6b", dtype: "auto", device: "auto", onProgress });
const [a] = await jev.decide(text, [noul("Is the name of God used here as a curse?")]);
a.probability;                  // p(yes), 0..1; a.answer is p >= 0.5; a.confidence is max(p, 1-p)
const [s] = await jev.decide(text, [score("How much of this is gossip?", ["none", "mild", "heavy"])]);
s.normalized;                   // expected level index scaled to 0..1 (the same meaning as askScore's p in prompt-backend.js)
```

- `decide(state, questions, options?)` takes several questions and answers them in **one forward pass**; `noul` answers use the fixed options `["no", "yes"]` and return p(yes); `score` returns the expected level index (`score`, `normalized`) and `level`; `choice` returns the argmax and all probabilities. Options: `temperature` (default 1 for kev), `maxStateTokens` (default 8192 for kev), `truncation` ("cut" or "error"). `OpenJev.info()` returns `isCached`, `downloadSize`, `files`, `device`, `dtype` without loading. `countTokens(text)`, `dispose()`. Concurrent `decide()` calls are queued. [verified] https://registry.npmjs.org/open-jev/-/open-jev-0.1.2.tgz (`README.md`, `dist/index.js`, source map)
- **Limits per model**: kev: 1 to 255 choice options, 2 to 255 score levels. [verified] same README

### 2.3 How the LoRA and the pointer head are applied in the browser build

- **They are not applied at run time.** `config.json` says `"lora": {"r": 16, "alpha": 32, "merged": true}`: the adapter is merged into the Qwen3 weights before export, and the pointer head (`head_dim` 256, `scale` 0.0625) is part of the graph, in fp32. The graph builds the block-causal mask, the segment ids and the positions itself from the five delimiter token ids, so the caller only sends `input_ids` and `attention_mask`. [verified] https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX/resolve/main/config.json and the card
- **What a question looks like on the wire**: `<state> state-tokens` then, per question, `<q> instruction-tokens` followed by `<opt> option-tokens </opt>` for each option and a closing `<decide>`. The model's score for an option is the value of `logits` at that option's `</opt>` position; a softmax within one question's options gives its distribution. A question sees the state and itself only (block-causal), so questions packed together get the probabilities they would get alone ("measured max delta 4e-6"). [verified] https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX (card) and the open-jev source
- **Delimiter token ids** (from `config.json`): state 151659 (`<|fim_prefix|>`), question 151660 (`<|fim_middle|>`), option start 151648 (`<|box_start|>`), option end 151649 (`<|box_end|>`), decide 151661 (`<|fim_suffix|>`). [verified]
- **Caller text is escaped** so it can never forge a delimiter: `<|name|>` becomes `<¦name¦>`. Choice options with descriptions render as `name: description`. [verified] same sources

### 2.4 Versions needed

- **open-jev 0.1.2** needs `@huggingface/transformers >=4.3.0`; the demo uses `^4.3.0`. [verified] https://registry.npmjs.org/open-jev
- **Transformers.js 4.3.2** (latest stable, published 2026-10-27, Apache-2.0; 4.4.0-next.1 is a pre-release) depends on `onnxruntime-web` **1.31.0-dev.20260914-8d85527a0** (a dev build, MIT), `onnxruntime-node` 1.30.0, `@huggingface/jinja` ^0.5.10 (MIT), `@huggingface/tokenizers` ^0.2.0 (Apache-2.0), `sharp` ^0.35.4 (not used in the browser). onnxruntime-web's own latest stable is 1.31.1 (2026-10-26), but the Transformers.js bundle embeds the dev build, and onnxruntime requires the JS and the WASM to come from the same build. [verified] https://registry.npmjs.org/@huggingface%2ftransformers , https://registry.npmjs.org/onnxruntime-web and https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/env-flags-and-session-options.html

### 2.5 Can the decision be reproduced without the package?

- **Yes.** The model card contains a complete recipe in about 30 lines using only Transformers.js (`AutoTokenizer`, `PreTrainedModel`, `Tensor`): escape the text, tokenize, build the sequence, run the graph, read `logits` at each `</opt>`, softmax. open-jev adds validation, a queue, truncation, device and dtype choice and `info()`. [verified] https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX
- **It needs neither open-jev nor Transformers.js in principle**: the same sequence goes into a plain onnxruntime-web `InferenceSession` with `externalData`, and any Qwen2-style BPE tokenizer that reads `tokenizer.json` (for example `@huggingface/tokenizers` 0.2.0, Apache-2.0, 361 KB unpacked). [verified: the algorithm; not verified: that `@huggingface/tokenizers` reproduces the ids, which a parity test must show]
- **Recommendation**: do not depend on `open-jev` at run time. It is a one-maintainer, two-version package whose `dist/index.js` has a bare `import ... from "@huggingface/transformers"` that does not resolve in an extension page without a bundler or an import map. Port the 120 lines that matter (sequence builder, escape, softmax, decoders) into a pure module of the project, with unit tests. [verified: bare imports in `dist/index.js`; my recommendation]
- **Pitfall found in a sibling port**: Transformers.js splits runs of spaces differently from the Rust tokenizer for Metaspace tokenizers (a Julia-1 port saw logits move by up to 3.3). Qwen3 uses byte-level BPE, not Metaspace, so it should not apply, but a parity test of token ids on the project's own titles (double spaces, emoji, non-ASCII) is cheap. [verified: the issue says so; not verified for Qwen3] https://github.com/nico-martin/open-jev/issues/2

## 3. Running it inside a Chrome MV3 extension

### 3.1 Content Security Policy

- **The default extension-page CSP is `script-src 'self'; object-src 'self';` and "WebAssembly will be disabled".** Chrome enforces a minimum of `script-src 'self' 'wasm-unsafe-eval'; object-src 'self'`, and adding `'unsafe-eval'` is rejected at install time. The manifest therefore needs `"content_security_policy": {"extension_pages": "script-src 'self' 'wasm-unsafe-eval'; object-src 'self';"}`. The current manifest has no `content_security_policy` key. [verified] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/manifest/content-security-policy and extension/manifest.json
- **A Transformers.js user hit exactly this**: "Refused to compile or instantiate WebAssembly module because neither 'wasm-eval' nor 'unsafe-eval' is an allowed source of script in the following Content Security Policy directive: script-src 'self'". [verified] https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/issues/839
- **A `new Function` call exists in the WebGPU loaders (risk).** `ort-wasm-simd-threaded.asyncify.mjs`, `.jsep.mjs`, `.jspi.mjs` and the self-contained `transformers.min.js` each contain one `new Function(...)` inside Emscripten's embind "method caller" (`emval`). `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` does not allow `new Function`; under the MV3 CSP it would throw if executed. The plain CPU loader `ort-wasm-simd-threaded.mjs` has none. My guess is that the call belongs to embind's `val::call`, which onnxruntime's WebNN provider would use and this project never selects, so it is probably unreachable on the WebGPU and WASM paths. [verified: the `new Function` strings in the files; not verified: the guess and that it is unreachable; only a run under the real CSP shows] https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/onnxruntime-web@1.31.0-dev.20260914-8d85527a0/dist/ort-wasm-simd-threaded.asyncify.mjs
- **No `eval(`, no `importScripts`, no `Blob` or `createObjectURL` in the three loaders.** `transformers.min.js` does contain `new Blob` and `createObjectURL`; in the code I read they serve the proxy worker loader and the WASM-factory cache (see 3.2). [verified: text search of the files]

[verified: searched the files of the npm tarball] https://registry.npmjs.org/@huggingface/transformers/-/transformers-4.3.2.tgz
- **The vendor list** (sizes and SHA-256 are mine, from the files I fetched; the tarball's sha512 equals the registry's `sha512-V3+dqf3iWlRYJ9I9GreM3zGQd0lAXnnoPeddmkOQYWAEE4l/+Fi6p7tx/qVUJlKQgLXGim2W76rTOzz+eC0i7g==`): [verified]
  - `transformers.min.js`: 587,917 bytes, sha256 `8b5a3171f8876bab2b1820a7d65eb87628e3d30bb5ea85b9dd398e76eb34decb`, Apache-2.0 (ships ONNX Runtime Web, MIT, inside).

- **The default wasm location is a CDN.** Transformers.js sets `wasmPaths` to `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/onnxruntime-web@<version>/dist/ort-wasm-simd-threaded.asyncify.{mjs,wasm}` unless it is already set or the context is a service worker. Set `env.backends.onnx.wasm.wasmPaths = { mjs: chrome.runtime.getURL(...), wasm: chrome.runtime.getURL(...) }` before the first session. [verified] `src/backends/onnx.js` in https://registry.npmjs.org/@huggingface/transformers/-/transformers-4.3.2.tgz
- **The CDN string stays in the file.** A Chrome Web Store reviewer rejected a Transformers.js 2.17.2 extension for "including remotely hosted code in a Manifest V3 item"; the author fixed it by bundling all ORT wasm files, and "Google has accepted the new version". Not a barrier for "Load unpacked", but for a store build patch the literal out and pin the patched file's hash. [verified: issue text] https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/issues/839
- **Blob import is already avoided inside extensions.** `loadWasmFactory` returns the original URL instead of a blob URL when `chrome.runtime.id` exists or in a service worker, because of "security restrictions on dynamic import() of blob URLs"; it still pre-fetches the wasm binary and tries to store it in the Cache API, and failures there are caught and only logged as warnings. Set `env.useWasmCache = false` to skip it. [verified: source, `src/backends/utils/cacheWasm.js` and `src/backends/onnx.js`; not verified: runtime behaviour in an extension]
- **Threads**: the asyncify build is single-threaded ("asyncify never spawns workers" in Transformers.js's own comment). Multi-threaded WASM needs `crossOriginIsolated`, which an extension can request with the manifest keys `cross_origin_embedder_policy` and `cross_origin_opener_policy` (Chrome 93+). ORT's docs: threads only when the browser supports them and cross-origin isolation is on; the proxy worker "cannot work with WebGPU EP" and "cannot work in a CSP restricted environment". A third party reports that threaded WASM "hangs silently inside a user-created worker and inside `env.wasm.proxy`", so run it on the main thread of the offscreen document. [verified: the statements exist; not verified: that they hold in an extension] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/manifest/cross-origin-embedder-policy , https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/env-flags-and-session-options.html and https://huggingface.co/nvkudva/laya-onnx-q8

### 3.3 Where inference should run

- **Not the service worker.** (a) Chrome stops a service worker after 30 s of inactivity, after a single request longer than 5 minutes, or when a `fetch()` response takes more than 30 s to arrive, so the loaded model would be lost again and again. (b) `import()` is "disallowed on ServiceWorkerGlobalScope by the HTML specification", which breaks onnxruntime's loader; the Transformers.js issue (#787, opened 2024-05-30) was still open with an unanswered "any updates?" on 2025-11-28. (c) WebGPU itself does work in extension service workers since Chrome 124, so the GPU is not the blocker. [verified] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/service-workers/lifecycle , https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/issues/787 and https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-124
- **Not the options page or the popup** for inference: they exist only while open. They are fine for starting the download and for showing status. [not verified: general knowledge; no source fetched]
- **Yes: an offscreen document.** `chrome.offscreen` (Chrome 109+, permission `offscreen`, no install warning) gives a hidden Window with DOM, one per extension, a static bundled HTML page, only the `chrome.runtime` API, and no lifetime limit except for the `AUDIO_PLAYBACK` reason (closes after 30 s without audio). `createDocument` needs a reason and a justification; no reason names machine-learning inference, so the honest choice is `WORKERS` (the downloader worker) with a justification that says the local model runs there. [verified] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/offscreen and https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/permissions-list
- **Messaging**: content scripts talk to the service worker (as `ai-ask` does today); the service worker creates the offscreen document on demand (`runtime.getContexts` finds an existing one) and forwards with a `target: 'offscreen'` field, because `runtime.sendMessage` reaches every extension page. [verified: the pattern is in the Chrome docs example] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/offscreen
- **One commenter says Chrome unloads an offscreen document "after a few minutes idle"**; the Chrome docs give no such limit (only `AUDIO_PLAYBACK` has one). Design for the document disappearing; the reload time from OPFS is unknown. [not verified: a reader comment] https://dev.to/sattyamjjain/i-built-a-chrome-extension-that-scans-websites-for-threats-using-ai-entirely-on-device-23kp
- **WebGPU inside an offscreen document is not documented by Chrome**; the document "is an instance of window". A third-party extension reports that its offscreen document "loads and runs the WebLLM engine. All AI inference happens here, using your GPU via WebGPU", and a Medium write-up (not readable here, 403) is said to do the same with Transformers.js. Must be shown on the owner's Mac with this runtime. [not verified: third-party reports, not reproduced] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/offscreen and the dev.to post above
- **Already available now**: the options page's System details row "Graphics" calls `navigator.gpu.requestAdapter()`; if it shows an adapter on the owner's Mac, WebGPU exists for extension pages. Adding `adapter.features.has('shader-f16')` and the limits is a two-line change. [verified: `collectMachine()` in extension/src/options/options.js]

### 3.4 Downloading the weights from huggingface.co

- **Stable, pinned URLs work**: `https://huggingface.co/<repo>/resolve/<commit sha>/<path>`. For the large files the server answers 302 to a signed CDN URL on `https://us.aws.cdn.hf.co/xet-bridge-us/...` (Xet storage); for small files a 307 to `/api/resolve-cache/...` on the same host. [verified: `curl -I` on 2026-11-26] Other CDN host names under `hf.co` may appear over time. [not verified: my expectation] https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX/resolve/5a827091b3d58fe4fdfc02a5a5b2ce364047d678/onnx/model_q4f16.onnx_data
- **The signed URL lives one hour** (`Expires` is 3,599 s after the `date` header). A resumed download must resolve the `resolve` URL again. [verified: computed from the response headers]
- **Range requests work**: `accept-ranges: bytes` on both hosts; `Range: bytes=0-15` on the CDN URL returned `206` with `content-range: bytes 0-15/335357952`. [verified: curl]
- **CORS is open for extension origins**: both the `resolve` endpoint (`access-control-allow-origin` echoes the `Origin`, preflight allows `range`) and the CDN (`access-control-allow-origin: *`, preflight allows `*`) answer for `Origin: chrome-extension://...`. So a `fetch` from an extension page with `Range` should work even without host permissions. With host permissions the extension origin is exempt from CORS anyway ("Extension origins aren't so limited"). [verified: curl with a fake extension origin; not verified: in a real browser] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/network-requests
- **Permissions**: `optional_host_permissions` for `https://huggingface.co/*` and `https://*.hf.co/*`, requested with `chrome.permissions.request()` from the Download click (a user gesture is required), avoids an install-time host warning for people who never download. `offscreen` and `unlimitedStorage` show no warning in Chrome's list. [verified] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/permissions and https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/permissions-list
- **Rate limit**: unauthenticated requests carry `ratelimit-policy: "fixed window";"resolvers";q=3000;w=300` (3,000 resolver requests per 300 s) and a warning to set `HF_TOKEN`. About 80 chunks of 4 MiB for the 335 MB file is far below that. [verified: response headers, 2026-11-26]
- **Transformers.js's own downloader is not enough for this project**: it reads each file into one `Uint8Array`, stores it in the Cache API under the remote URL, has no `Range` or resume code, and no checksum check (SHA-256 appears only in an experimental cross-origin storage cache). [verified: source] `src/utils/hub.js`, `src/utils/cache.js` in https://registry.npmjs.org/@huggingface/transformers/-/transformers-4.3.2.tgz
- **It can read from a custom store**: `env.useCustomCache = true` with `env.customCache = { match(url), put(url, response) }`, keyed by the remote URL `https://huggingface.co/<repo>/resolve/<revision>/<file>` (pass `revision: '<commit>'` to pin it); `env.remoteHost` and `env.remotePathTemplate` can point at a mirror. [verified: source]

### 3.5 Storage: OPFS, Cache API or IndexedDB

- **`unlimitedStorage` "provides an unlimited quota for chrome.storage.local, IndexedDB, Cache Storage, and Origin Private File System"** and "exempts extensions from both quota restrictions and eviction"; without it the Chromium origin quota is up to 60 percent of the disk, and `navigator.storage.persist()` also protects against eviction. Extension storage is shared by the service worker, extension pages and offscreen documents, and is not cleared by "clear browsing data". [verified] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/permissions-list , https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/storage-and-cookies and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria
- **OPFS is the best fit** for a resumable download: byte-level writes at an offset with `createSyncAccessHandle()` ("a set of synchronous calls ... that can be run inside web workers only"), real files, and the same store is visible to the offscreen document and the options page. MDN describes OPFS as "low-level, byte-by-byte file access" without the security checks and temporary-file writes of the user-visible file system. [verified: MDN] https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/File_System_API/Origin_private_file_system ; [not verified: OPFS inside an extension origin was not tested; Chrome's permission text names it]
- **Cache API**: what Transformers.js uses by default (cache name `transformers-cache`, keyed by URL); a stored response cannot be appended to, so no resume; deletion by `caches.delete()`; usable in the service worker. [verified: Transformers.js source; Chrome docs say Cache Storage and IndexedDB are reachable from service workers] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/storage-and-cookies
- **IndexedDB**: possible with chunks of a few MB per record; more code than OPFS for the same result. [not verified: my judgement]
- **ONNX Runtime's own advice**: "cache the model by using the Cache API or Origin private file system"; `externalData` accepts "a URL, a Blob or a Uint8Array". The 2 GB `ArrayBuffer` limit in Chrome ("0x7fe00000 bytes") and the 4 GB WebAssembly limit do not matter for 335 MB; they matter for the 4B build. [verified: documentation, which may be dated] https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/large-models.html
- **The owner's disk**: `df` on 2026-11-26 shows 22 GiB free of 228 GiB (90 percent used). A 346 MB download fits, but it is worth showing the size before the click. [verified: `df -h`]

### 3.6 Verifying the SHA-256

- **WebCrypto cannot stream**: "this API does not support streaming input: you must read the entire input into memory". Options: (a) after the download, read the OPFS file and call `crypto.subtle.digest('SHA-256', buffer)` once (peak plus 335 MB inside a worker, simplest); (b) an incremental pure-JS SHA-256 while the chunks are written: `@noble/hashes` 2.4.0 (MIT, no dependencies, `sha256.create().update(a).update(b).digest()`, "2.8KB (gzipped)"). [verified] https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SubtleCrypto/digest and https://registry.npmjs.org/@noble%2fhashes
- **Pin everything in a manifest inside the extension**: repo, commit, path, size, SHA-256 (the values in 1.2). Verify every file after the download and before the first load; on mismatch delete and report. HF's `x-linked-etag` is the same server's claim and is not an independent check. [verified: header exists; my design]

### 3.7 Resume and delete

- **Resume**: keep the OPFS file and its current size; ask `resolve` for a fresh redirect (the signed URL expires in an hour); send `Range: bytes=<size>-`; require `206` and a `content-range` that starts at `<size>`; append; at the end verify the hash. Download in a dedicated worker inside the offscreen document so closing the options tab does not stop it. [verified: Range support; design is mine]
- **Delete**: `navigator.storage.getDirectory()` then `removeEntry('kev-0.6b', {recursive: true})`, then close the offscreen document; show `navigator.storage.estimate()` before and after. [not verified: the call sequence was not run in an extension origin]

### 3.8 Chrome Web Store policy (only if a store build is ever wanted)

- Chrome's extension AI guidance: "For larger, client-side models, trigger the download after the extension is installed. Manage the life cycle of your model independently of your extension ... Note that models are not considered remote hosted code." Remote hosted code "is anything that is executed by the browser that is loaded from someplace other than the extension's own files. Things like JavaScript and WASM. It does not include data or things like JSON or CSS." So the runtime and wasm must be inside the package; weights may be downloaded. [verified] https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/ai and https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/migrate/remote-hosted-code
- A reviewer could still call an ONNX graph "logic"; no store text names ML models. [not verified: only a submission settles it] plan.md section 3.9

## 4. Cost on a small machine

- **Latency, WebGPU, Chrome, MacBook Pro M3 Pro (with `shader-f16`)**: a third party measured open-jev with the demos, five states times four questions, tab in the foreground, warm-up excluded, about 40 state tokens: `kev-0.6b` q4f16 averages 109 ms (97 to 123) for email triage, 112 ms (106 to 116) for moderation, 115 ms (102 to 140) for lead scoring and 140 ms (132 to 155) for ticket routing; `kev-4b` 486 to 633 ms. The author thinks the small passes are "bound by per-kernel dispatch overhead rather than FLOPs". "No ORT warnings or WASM fallback in the console". "Peak memory isn't exposed by the demo, so it isn't reported here." [verified: as reported by a third party; not reproduced] https://github.com/nico-martin/open-jev/issues/1
- **For the project**: a feed title (about 40 characters) and a rule question (median 137 characters in the shipped rules) make a short sequence, so each ask should land near that range on an M3 Pro; **no published number exists for an 8 GB M1 or M2 class chip**. [verified: lengths from the shipped rules; not verified: transfer of the timing]
- **Latency, WebAssembly: no published numbers for Kev-0.6B.** The nearest datum is a different model: the Laya int8 encoder on WASM with 8 threads on Apple silicon takes about 290 ms at 43 tokens, 920 ms at 195 and 2.4 s at 512, and "falls back to a single thread and gets roughly 6x slower" without cross-origin isolation. The asyncify build used by Transformers.js is single-threaded. Treat the WASM path as "works, slow" until measured. [verified: the card says so; different model] https://huggingface.co/nvkudva/laya-onnx-q8
- **Server-side numbers exist but are not the browser**: the Kev card quotes 0.12 s per five-question request for Kev-0.6B on Apple Silicon (the machine is not named there; the README says Macs are served through MLX); the README quotes, on an M5 with 32 GB, five questions about a ~270-token text: Kev-0.8B 149 ms new text, 28 ms same text; Kev-4B 721 ms and 136 ms. [verified] https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/docs/model-cards/kev-0.6b-qwen3.md and https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev#readme
- **Memory: no published number for Kev-0.6B in a browser tab.** Facts that bound it: Transformers.js reads the 335 MB data file into one `Uint8Array` while loading (verified in its source). My reading, not measured: onnxruntime then copies it into the WebAssembly heap and, for WebGPU, into GPU buffers, and Apple Silicon has one memory pool, so the weights may exist two or three times for a short time. An arXiv paper (May 2026) says Transformers.js's steady-state memory is higher than other frameworks "in part by how it creates temporary copies of models into CPU buffers before sending them to GPU buffers" (Llama 3.2 1B f16 on Chrome with an Apple M4 Pro: LlamaWeb used 16 percent less than Transformers.js; absolute numbers only in a figure); a blog lists "Qwen3 0.6B is a 335MB download needing ~1.4GB of VRAM", which is WebLLM's estimate for weights plus a KV cache at a 4096-token context, a different runtime and a generator, so only a loose upper reference. [verified: the sources say so; not verified: any figure for Kev] https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20706v1 and https://pinggy.io/blog/run_llm_in_browser_webgpu/
- **Attention memory grows with the square of the length** (the graph materialises the score tensor): 16 heads times L squared times 2 bytes at fp16 (4 bytes at fp32) is 33.6 MB at L=1,024, 134 MB at 2,048, 537 MB at 4,096 and 2.1 GB at 8,192, per layer, as a transient. The 128 MiB default WebGPU storage-binding limit is reached at L=2,048 (fp16). Keep the whole sequence at or below about 1,024 tokens, which also matches the trained range. [verified: arithmetic on the traced graph and the 16 heads in `config.json`; not measured]
- **Limits that do not bite at this size**: WebAssembly 4 GB, Chrome `ArrayBuffer` about 2 GB, protobuf 2 GB. [verified: documented] https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/large-models.html
- **Apple Silicon 8 GB problems: none found for this model.** Related reports: onnxruntime-web WebGPU can hang after a device loss (an issue and a fix PR exist; those were not reachable here: `github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime` answered 404 on 2026-11-26); an iOS Safari crash on a 900-token prefill. Plan for `device lost` by timing out, closing the offscreen document and reloading. [not verified] https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/pull/33965 (found by search only)
- **Gemini Nano on the same Mac, for contrast**: plan.md 3.3 measured only `availability()`; Chrome documents 22 GB free disk and 16 GB RAM or more than 4 GB VRAM. ## 5. Proposal for this extension

Everything in this section is my design, not a finding; the facts it rests on carry their own tags in sections 1 to 4. Nothing here is built.

### 5.1 Files to add (nothing here is written yet)

- **`extension/vendor/transformers/transformers.min.js`**, **`extension/vendor/ort/ort-wasm-simd-threaded.asyncify.mjs`** and **`.wasm`**, with `LICENSE` files and a `vendor/README` that lists version, source URL and SHA-256 (3.2). A small `tools/vendor-kev.mjs` fetches them from the npm tarball and jsDelivr and refuses a hash mismatch.
- **`extension/src/ai/kev-core.js`** (pure, no browser API, unit-tested in Node like the other `core` modules): `escapeUserText`, `buildSequence({state, questions, delimiters, maxLength})` returning `inputIds` and the `</opt>` indices, `softmax`, `decodeNoul`, `decodeScore`. Delimiter ids come from the pinned `config.json`.
- **`extension/src/ai/kev-manifest.js`**: repo, commit, per-file size and SHA-256 for both sets, delimiter ids, `maxStateTokens` (384) and `maxLength` (1,024).
- **`extension/src/ai/kev-offscreen.html`** and **`kev-offscreen.js`**: imports the vendored bundle, sets `env.allowLocalModels = false`, `env.useWasmCache = false`, `env.backends.onnx.wasm.wasmPaths` to the vendored URLs and a custom cache that serves OPFS files; detects WebGPU and `shader-f16` (q4f16 set), otherwise q4 on WASM; loads on first use; answers `kev-ask`; closes after an idle time.
- **`extension/src/ai/kev-download-worker.js`**: streaming download into OPFS with `createSyncAccessHandle`, `Range` resume, incremental SHA-256, progress messages.
- **`extension/src/ai/kev-backend.js`**: the service-worker client with the same surface as `prompt-backend.js`: `getAvailability()`, `askNoul(question, text)` returning `{p}` or `{p: null, error}`, `askScore(question, levels, text)`, `resetSession()`; it creates the offscreen document and forwards messages.
- **Manifest**: `permissions` add `offscreen` and `unlimitedStorage`; `optional_host_permissions` `https://huggingface.co/*` and `https://*.hf.co/*`; `content_security_policy.extension_pages` as in 3.1. `minimum_chrome_version` 138 already covers `offscreen` (109) and `runtime.getContexts` (116).
- **Settings** (`extension/src/shared/config.js`): `ai.backend` (`'auto'` | `'prompt'` | `'kev'`) and `ai.kev` (`{ maxStateTokens: 384 }`).
- **Tests**: `tests/kev-core.test.js` (sequence layout, indices for the card's example, escape, softmax, noul and score decoding) and extensions of `tests/modelcheck.test.js`.

### 5.2 How it plugs into the existing code

- **Today**: `content/main.js` builds a thin `backend` whose `askNoul`/`askScore` send `{type: 'ai-ask', ...}` to the service worker; the worker answers from `ai/prompt-backend.js` after `aiAvailability()` says `available`; `refreshAi()` polls `ai-status` every 60 s. The engine only asks inside the uncertain band, caches by `rule.id + hash(text)`, and takes `p` from `{p}`. [verified] extension/src/content/main.js, extension/src/background/service-worker.js, extension/src/core/engine.js
- **Change**: in the service worker, `ai-status` and `ai-ask` pick the backend from `settings.ai.backend` (`auto` = Gemini Nano if `available`, else Kev if ready). `ai-status` also returns `backend` and a `loading` state so tabs re-poll quickly while the model loads. The engine cache must be cleared when the backend changes (`main.js` already clears it when `enabled` flips). The existing message handler does not look at the sender (except for `badge`); the new `kev-*` messages for download and delete should accept only senders whose `sender.url` is an extension page, never a content script (an options page opened in a tab also has `sender.tab`, so `sender.tab` alone does not tell them apart). [not verified: `sender.url` behaviour for content scripts was not tested]
- **`p` is not the same thing in the two backends**: Gemini Nano gives a vote fraction (0, 1/3, 2/3, 1); Kev gives a continuous probability. `flagAt` levels are calibrated per backend, which is what plan.md section 8 ("offline fit") already says. [verified: `askNoul` in prompt-backend.js and plan.md section 8]
- **Batching option for later**: Kev answers many questions about one text in one pass; the engine currently asks rule by rule. A `backend.askMany(text, questions)` would cut cost when several rules fire on the same title. [verified: open-jev `decide` takes an array]

### 5.3 The download UI (options page, On-device AI pane)

- **Where**: `modelcheck.js` already defines `CAPABILITIES` (`text`, `image`, `audio`) and a pure `statusInfo(availability, id)` returning label, tone, `canDownload` and an explanation; `renderModels()` draws one `article.model` per capability with a `Download` button and a `progress` element. Add a fourth row, "Local model (Kev-0.6B)", drawn from a new pure `kevStatusInfo(state)` so it is testable. [verified: extension/src/core/modelcheck.js, extension/src/options/options.js lines 465-530]
- **States and controls**: Not downloaded (button "Download 346 MB", the licence line "Apache-2.0, English only", the note "stored in this browser, nothing is sent"); Downloading (bar with MB done of total and speed; Pause and Cancel); Verifying ("Checking SHA-256 of 3 files"); Ready ("WebGPU, q4f16" or "WebAssembly, q4: slow"); Error (with the failing file and a Retry); Delete ("Delete model files", two-step button, then the freed size from `navigator.storage.estimate()`).
- **Model choice**: a drop-down with one entry now (Kev-0.6B). Do not offer Kev-4B (2.3 GB) on a small machine. Show the detected path (WebGPU with or without `shader-f16`, or WASM) and download only the matching set.
- **Honesty line**: "On this computer the model runs on the graphics chip / on the processor (slow)". Add `shader-f16` and `maxBufferSize` to the System details rows. [verified: row structure in `machineRows`]
- **Permission step**: the Download click first calls `chrome.permissions.request` for the two hosts, then starts the worker; because the request needs the click's user activation, do it before any `await`.

### 5.4 Order of work

0. **Today, no code**: open the options page, On-device AI, System details, and read the Graphics row on the owner's Mac. [verified: the page exists]
1. **Spike A, half a day to a day (throwaway extension)**: manifest with the CSP, vendored bundle, offscreen document, load the q4f16 files from `http://127.0.0.1` or from huggingface.co, answer 20 titles. Record: console errors (especially `new Function` / EvalError), `navigator.gpu` and `shader-f16` in the offscreen document, load time, first and warm `decide` time, memory in Chrome's Task Manager and Activity Monitor, the same on WASM q4. **Go or no-go.** [not verified until done]
2. **Spike B, half a day, can run in parallel (Node, no browser)**: score the project's examples (5.7). **Go or no-go on accuracy** before any UI.
3. **Build the pure core and the offscreen backend** (`kev-core.js` with tests, offscreen document, backend client, service-worker routing, settings): about one day.
4. **Build the downloader** (OPFS, resume, SHA-256, delete) **and the options row** with tests: about two days.
5. **End-to-end in real Chrome** with the existing CDP tooling (`tools/e2e.py`): download from a local server that serves the pinned files, kill the connection half-way and resume, corrupt a byte and see the hash refuse it, delete, reload; then the same against huggingface.co once. Headless Chrome may have no WebGPU, so expect the WASM path there: about one day.
6. **Install page and README**: say that the first use downloads 346 MB (383 MB on the WASM path) and where it is stored.

### 5.5 Honest estimate

All durations are my estimates from the size of the work in sections 3 and 5.1 [not verified]; none was measured.

- **Spikes**: half a day to a day each (A is the one that can fail).
- **Smallest useful version**: Transformers.js's own downloader into the Cache API with a progress bar and a delete button, no resume, no hash check: about one day after a successful spike A. Not recommended as the end state, because a corrupted or truncated 335 MB file would be trusted.
- **The version asked for** (resumable, SHA-256 pinned, delete, status UI, tests, an end-to-end run): **several days, about 4 to 6 working days**, on top of the spikes. If spike A shows that WebGPU does not work under the extension CSP, add 1 to 2 days for a WASM-only path that is slow.
- **Accuracy work is separate** and may be the larger part: calibrating `flagAt` per rule on the labelled set, and, if zero-shot is weak, a fine-tune (the third-party example took 1 h 49 min on Apple Silicon for 8,347 records; on an 8 GB Mac that is untested). [not verified: estimates are mine]

### 5.6 Risks

The ranking and the mitigations are my assessment [not verified]; the facts behind each risk are tagged where they appear above.

- **Accuracy** (see 1.6): policy-like questions are the weakest area of a 0.6B Kev; a third party's zero-shot yes/no results were 55 to 57 percent (on its own questions, in 33 languages). Mitigation: Spike B first; keep the lexicon stage and the uncertain-band cascade, so Kev never decides alone; thresholds from the labelled set; fine-tune only if needed.
- **MV3 CSP and the `new Function` in the loaders**: probably dead code, not proven. Mitigation: spike A; the plain CPU loader has no such call.
- **WebGPU in an offscreen document on an 8 GB Mac**: undocumented, no memory figures. Mitigation: spike A, an idle timeout that closes the document, a cap of about 1,024 tokens, `device lost` handling.
- **Memory spikes** while loading (a 335 MB buffer plus copies). Mitigation: load only when a YouTube tab asks; close after idle; do not load during a download.
- **WASM fallback speed** unknown, probably seconds per ask on one thread. Mitigation: use it only for low-volume checks (watch page), or not at all.
- **Supply chain**: the weights live in a community repo (`onnx-community`) and could change or vanish. Mitigation: pin the commit and SHA-256 in the extension; consider a mirror on the project's own site or a GitHub release (host permission, `remoteHost` setting).
- **Vendored runtime drift**: Transformers.js pins an onnxruntime dev build; the JS and WASM must match. Mitigation: vendor exact files with hashes; update both together.
- **Store policy** (only for a store build): remote hosted code finding for the CDN string; offscreen reason mismatch. Not an issue for "Load unpacked".
- **Idle offscreen document reports** and **service-worker restarts**: the model state must be rebuildable from OPFS at any time.
- **Licence of training data** not analysed; Apache-2.0 for the weights.

### 5.7 Checking accuracy on the project's own examples (before anyone relies on it)

- **Material**: the shipped rules have 46 rules (40 text, 6 picture or sound), 126 `example:` lines and 59 `spared:` lines, 45 `ask noul` and 1 `ask score` questions; titles are 21 to 74 characters (median 41), questions 71 to 259 characters (median 137). Kev reads text only, so the 6 media rules are out. [verified: parsed with the project's own `extension/src/core/rules.js`]
- **How the project already tests them**: `tests/rule-examples.test.js` runs every example and spared title through the real engine with a stand-in model answer and checks that a model-dependent rule hands an example to the model (it holds a cue word) and does not flag a spared title when the model says no. So the part that needs a real answer is exactly the cue-word titles. [verified: header comment of tests/rule-examples.test.js]
- **Harness (Node, no browser)**: `tools/eval-kev.mjs` outside the extension, with Transformers.js and onnxruntime-node installed in a scratch folder (the repo has no dependencies and should keep none). For each text rule: positives are its `example:` lines, negatives are its `spared:` lines plus a hand-checked neutral set (an example of another rule is not automatically negative: "Jesus Christ, what the fuck was that" is an example of `lords_name` only, but the word lists of `coarse_words` also hit it at 0.95; checked with the project's engine). State is `[title, channel].join('\n')` as `engine.textFor` builds it; the question is the rule's `ask noul` text. Report per rule: p for every item, AUC, precision and recall at the rule's own `flagAt` (0.80 for the first rules; read it from each rule) and at 0.5, and the share of confident errors (p at least 0.9 and wrong).
- **Variants to try** (each is a one-line change): question phrased as a question or as a statement (open-jev's demo uses both), title only or title plus channel, state cut at 384 or 1,024 tokens.
- **Baselines to beat** (plan.md section 8 selection rule): lexicon alone (what the engine does without a model) and Gemini Nano's three-wording vote on the same items if `availability()` is `available` on that machine.
- **Parity**: run a few dozen items through Node q4 (CPU) and through the browser q4f16 (WebGPU) and compare `p`; a third party saw 0.001 between q4 and q4f16 on a fine-tune, Kev's own README sees up to 0.03 for bf16 against fp32 on a GPU. [verified: statements; not verified for this model]
- **What this can and cannot show**: the 185 lines are the rule author's illustrations, not an independent labelled set, so this is a smoke test for "is Kev clearly better than the lexicon alone on cue-word titles". Real operating points need the labelled set of plan.md section 9, with a fit part and a held-out part.
