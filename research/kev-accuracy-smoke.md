# Kev-0.6B on the rule author's own examples: a smoke test

Runs of 2026-11-26 with `tools/eval-kev.mjs`: the first on the plain questions with the engine as it was in the morning, the second (the section "Examples inside the question") after the engine learned to put examples beside the question and to let a model overrule a strong word-list hit. This is a smoke test on the rule author's illustrations, NOT a labelled test set: the `example:` and `spared:` lines were written to show what a rule means, by the person who wrote the rule, and the word lists were tuned beside them. It can show whether the model separates "caught" from "spared" at all. It cannot give an error rate for real YouTube titles.

How to read the tags
- **[measured]**: produced by the run described here, on this machine (MacBook Air, Apple M2, 8 GB), and repeatable with the commands at the end.
- **[not measured]**: not run; the reason follows.

## What was run

- **Model**: the pinned Kev-0.6B files (commit 5a827091), the 4-bit `q4` set (the one the WebAssembly path uses), on the processor through onnxruntime-node 1.30.0 inside Transformers.js 4.3.2. **[measured]**
- **Same numbers as the reference package**: the sequence layout and the decoding of `extension/src/ai/kev-core.js` give probabilities that equal those of the open-jev 0.1.2 package to the last digit on 10 inputs (the card's own example, titles from the rules, delimiter look-alikes such as `<|fim_prefix|>`, emoji, double spaces, a one-character text, a text cut at 384 tokens, and two ordinal questions with 3 and 5 levels): largest difference 0. The five delimiter strings encode to the ids of the manifest with the real tokenizer. **[measured]**
- **Material**: every text rule that has a question and reads feed cards: 49 rules, 149 `example:` lines (what the rule should catch) and 79 `spared:` lines (what looks similar and must be left alone). The rule files were being edited by another session while this ran (the research of the same morning counted 46 rules, 126 examples and 59 spared lines); these are the numbers for the files as they were during the run. **[measured]**
- **Text given to the model**: as `engine.textFor` builds it for a feed card, title and channel joined by a line break; the lines have no channel, so it is the title alone. The longest whole sequence (title, question, options, delimiters) is 93 tokens, so the 384-token cut of the state never applied. **[measured]**
- **Question**: the rule's own `ask noul` text (a question, 71 to 259 characters, with its "and not ..." exceptions), asked for EVERY line, and then the real `Engine` with this model as its backend, so that the engine decides for itself when to ask (only inside the uncertain band below the rule's `flag at` level, where a word-list cue is present). **[measured]**
- **Baseline**: the lexicon alone is the real engine without a model (what the extension does today without Gemini Nano). Gemini Nano's three-wording vote could not be run: the Prompt API exists only inside Chrome, and on this machine `availability()` answers "downloadable" (plan.md section 3.3). **[not measured]**
- **Speed**: the whole run (loading the model, 243 model calls, the engine) took 21.7 s on the 8 cores of the M2 (the processor was busy on about 3.6 of them). **[measured]**

## Result: the question as the rule writes it

- **Words alone**: 79 of the 149 examples are caught, 0 of the 79 spared lines are flagged. **[measured]**
- **Words plus Kev**: 95 of the 149 examples are caught (16 more), 2 of the 79 spared lines are flagged. **[measured]**
  - The model is asked on 90 lines (70 examples, 20 spared): the lines where a cue is present but below the rule's level. Of the 70 examples that the words miss, Kev flags 16 (23 percent). Of the 20 spared lines in the band, it flags 2 (10 percent): "A priest answers the new atheism: a lecture" (p 0.86, rule denial_of_god) and "Is Spiritism compatible with the Catholic faith? A priest answers" (p 0.82, rule spiritism); both are Catholic answers to a claim, which is exactly what the questions' exceptions name. The next three are just below the level: "The history of April Fools' Day" 0.76, "How to answer Jehovah's Witnesses at your door: a Catholic guide" 0.68, "Why Protestants become Catholic: a convert's story" 0.62. **[measured]**
  - The engine, as it was during this first run, only asked the model below the flag level, so the model could ADD flags but never remove one that the words made. (Rules with `confirm: model` came later the same day: the section below.) **[measured: engine.js at that time]**
- **How well the model separates the two kinds of line** (AUC: the chance that a random example gets a higher p than a random spared line; 0.5 is a coin):
  - on the 90 lines that the engine would ask: 0.62, 95 percent interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples 0.48 to 0.74, so it includes chance. **[measured]**
  - on all 228 lines with the model asked every time: 0.73, interval 0.66 to 0.79. Most of this comes from lines that the words already settle (the rule author wrote examples with strong cues). **[measured]**
- **Per rule**: the model adds examples without adding a false alarm in 9 of 49 rules and adds a false alarm in 2; most rules have 3 examples and 1 or 2 spared lines, so a single rule says nothing. **[measured]**
- **Confident mistakes of the model alone** (p of at least 0.9 on a spared line, or at most 0.1 on an example): 15 of 228 lines. Examples: "The Hell of Dante: a lecture" for the curse rule (0.92); "Does God exist? Five ways of St. Thomas Aquinas" for the denial rule (0.91); "Religion is a scam, change my mind" for the mockery rule (0.05). **[measured]**

## Variants of the research (section 5.7), and one more

- **The question as a statement** (opening turned into a statement where the question starts with "Is this", "Does this" or "Does the speaker"; the 29 questions of other shapes stay as written): worse. Catches 99 examples but flags 4 spared lines; AUC 0.59 on the asked lines (0.43 to 0.73) and 0.66 on all lines (0.58 to 0.73); at p of at least 0.5 the model alone flags 38 of 79 spared lines, against 18 for the question form. The answers shift upward by about 0.1 to 0.2. **[measured]**
- **384 against 1,024 tokens of state**: no difference is possible with this material: the whole sequence is at most 93 tokens, the state was never cut. The setting matters for descriptions and subtitle windows, for which the project has no labelled lines. **[measured: no cut; the effect on long text is not measured]**
- **Extra, not in the research: the question without its exceptions** (everything from ", and not ..." or ", rather than ..." on is cut): slightly better. Catches 94 examples and flags 0 of 79 spared lines; AUC 0.70 on the asked lines (0.57 to 0.83) and 0.78 on all lines (0.72 to 0.84). The exceptions are what the rule author uses to describe the spared cases, and a 0.6B model does not seem to use them. This is a hint for how to word questions for this model, not a change to any rule. **[measured]**
- **Title plus channel**: not measured; the lines have no channel.
- **Browser `q4f16` against Node `q4`**: not measured in this step; it is part of the browser spike.

## What this means

- **Verdict: slightly better than the lexicon alone on the cue-word titles, not clearly.** On the lines that matter (cue present, words undecided) the signal is weak: 0.62, an interval that includes chance. In numbers, with the rule's own thresholds: 16 more of 70 missed examples (+23 percent) at the price of 2 false alarms in 20 spared lines (10 percent). **[measured]**
- (This verdict is for the plain question. With the examples in the question, in the form that replaced the first one, the picture is clearer: see the section below.)
- It is not at chance on the whole set and it is not worse than the words; the layered design (Kev first, Chrome's model as a second look when Kev is unsure, words first of all) is as safe as before, because a model can only add flags and the rule's own level decides.
- **Calibration matters more than the model here**: for many true examples Kev says 0.2 to 0.5 (below the rule levels of 0.75 to 0.85) and for some spared lines it says 0.8 to 0.9. No threshold separates them cleanly; the fitted levels of plan.md section 8 (on a labelled set) are needed before the model's answers are trusted. Thresholds were not changed.
- **What it cannot tell**: how it does on real titles with channel names, on descriptions or on subtitles; whether Chrome's model is better; whether the 4-bit quantisation costs accuracy (no full-precision graph is published).
- The owner wants to try it on the rule examples himself: the settings page gets a panel ("Check on the rule examples") that does this run in the browser, with the real engine and the layered models (see research/kev-progress.md).

## Examples inside the question, and `confirm: model` (2026-11-26, later the same day)

The engine changed after the run above, in two ways (core/engine.js, core/shots.js, rules key `confirm: model`): a rule may let the model OVERRULE a strong word-list hit (a clear "no", below 0.35, takes the hit away; used by the four ideology rules, so that a Catholic warning about Freemasonry is not flagged), and every yes/no question now carries the rule's own nearest `example:` and `spared:` lines. The same 228 lines (49 rules, 149 examples, 79 spared lines, rule files as of that moment) were run again with `tools/eval-kev.mjs --grid`, the model on the processor (`q4`, onnxruntime-node), **every line judged with itself left out of the examples that the model is shown** (leave-one-out: it never sees its own answer). "Focus" means the four rules with `confirm: model` (Freemasonry, Communism, Modernism, Spiritism: 14 examples and 15 spared lines, the spared ones being Catholic warnings). "Wrong" counts the lines whose verdict is wrong: examples not flagged plus spared lines flagged; the word lists alone get 70 of the 228 wrong (79 of 149 examples flagged, no spared line flagged).

- **No examples** (the plain question): the engine flags 94 examples and 2 spared lines, 57 wrong; the model alone separates the two kinds with an AUC of 0.727 (95 percent interval 0.66 to 0.79). Focus: 9 of 14 examples, 1 of 15 spared lines, 6 wrong. **[measured]**
- **Examples after the question** (the first format of core/shots.js, "For example, yes: ...; For example, no: ..."), 2, 3 and 5 of each side: AUC 0.752, 0.742, 0.739; 56, 57, 57 wrong; the paired change of the AUC against no examples is -0.016 to +0.069, -0.028 to +0.058 and -0.032 to +0.054: indistinguishable from nothing. **[measured]**
- **Other formats at 3 of each side**: a bulleted list under two headings: AUC 0.728, 59 wrong (focus 8 wrong); the examples alternating, each with its answer ("... -> yes"): AUC 0.673, 49 wrong but 7 spared lines flagged (it answers yes more often, and its AUC is lower). **[measured]**
- **Examples first, the question last** ("Examples that count (yes): ...", "Examples that do not count (no): ...", "Question: ..."): clearly better, at every number of examples:
  - 1 of each side: AUC 0.789, 33 wrong; focus 12 of 14 examples, 0 of 15 spared lines flagged. **[measured]**
  - **2 of each side: AUC 0.815 (interval 0.76 to 0.87), 129 of 149 examples and 5 of 79 spared lines flagged, 25 wrong; the paired change of the AUC against no examples is +0.025 to +0.151 (the interval leaves 0). Focus: 13 of 14 examples flagged, 0 of 15 spared lines flagged, 1 wrong.** **[measured]**
  - 3 of each side: AUC 0.787, 28 wrong, focus 1 spared line flagged (3 wrong); 4: AUC 0.785, 29 wrong; 5: AUC 0.781, 30 wrong, focus 3 of 15 spared lines flagged (5 wrong). More examples are not better. **[measured]**
- **What the best one (2 of each side, examples first) gets right and wrong on the Catholic warnings the owner cares about**: none of the 5 spared Freemasonry lines is flagged (the four that the model is asked about get 0.15, 0.13, 0.39 and 0.61 against a level of 0.8; "Stone masonry" has no cue and is not asked), nor any spared line of Communism, Modernism or Spiritism. Of the 14 examples of the four rules it flags 13; the one it does not is "What is Freemasonry? A lodge member explains" (0.64). Asked on its own about every line, the model would say yes to "The Cold War explained" (0.92); what spares that line is that the word lists find no cue in it and the engine does not ask the model. The 5 spared lines of all rules that are flagged are Catholic answers to a claim ("A priest answers the new atheism: a lecture" 0.97, "How to answer Jehovah's Witnesses at your door: a Catholic guide" 0.94, "Anti-Catholic bigotry in America: a history" 0.90) and two cue-word look-alikes of the name rules ("Renan vs Neymar: football highlights of the week" 0.97, "Zola Budd: the runner's story" 0.90): a model that is shown examples answers yes more readily. **[measured]**
- The format was changed in core/shots.js to the examples-first form, and the default is 2 examples of each side (a CHOICE: one number for all rules).
- **How far to trust this**: the best of eleven configurations, chosen on the very lines on which it is measured, is optimistic; that the eight "examples first" configurations (AUC 0.78 to 0.82) all beat the three "after" ones (0.73 to 0.75) says the format matters, not only the pick. The lines are the rule author's illustrations and many of them are near copies of each other (each rule has 3 to 5 examples that were written together), so a title that is NOT a paraphrase of a line of the rule will do worse than these numbers. The titles that the owner is going to add (real titles of channels he knows, books from the Index) are the test that matters; the panel on the settings page counts them as soon as they are in the rule.
- **Cost**: examples make the question longer (the longest whole sequence: 93 tokens with none, 165 with 2 of each side, 219 with 5). Measured in the extension in a real Chrome, with the examples-first form and 2 of each side: the whole "Check on the rule examples" (228 lines, 103 of them asked) took 22 to 25 s on WebGPU (`q4f16`) and 6 min 56 s on WebAssembly (`q4`), which is 4.0 s per question there (1.8 s for the plain questions of gate B). In the browser the result was 127 examples flagged and 6 spared lines on WebGPU, 128 and 6 on WebAssembly, against 129 and 5 in Node on the processor with `q4`: the same picture, with the small differences of a few hundredths in p that the runtimes have (research/kev-progress.md). **[measured]**

## Commands

- Install the dependencies in a scratch folder, never in the repository: `npm install --ignore-scripts @huggingface/transformers@4.3.2` (and `open-jev@0.1.2` for the cross-check).
- Put the pinned files in a folder as `onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX/{config.json,tokenizer.json,tokenizer_config.json,onnx/model_q4.onnx,onnx/model_q4.onnx_data}`; the two `onnx/` files must be real files or hard links (ONNX Runtime refuses symbolic links for the weights).
- `KEV_DEPS=<scratch>/node_modules KEV_MODEL=<folder> node tools/eval-kev.mjs --variant question|statement|short --out result.json`
- The manifest against Hugging Face: `node tools/check-kev-manifest.mjs --full` (streams about 710 MB, keeps nothing).
