# Vianney Content Blocker

A browser extension, mainly for YouTube, that hides content you decided against. It applies rules you write in plain text: word lists decide first, a small local model (Kev-0.6B, downloaded once from the settings page) and Chrome's built-in on-device model are optional second opinions, asked in turn, and subtitles are checked for words and passages you want muted or skipped. The name and the starting idea come from St. Jean-Marie Vianney, the Curé of Ars, on guarding the eyes and the ears.

**Status:** version 0.4 is built and ready to test (Chrome 138 or newer; Firefox 140 or newer and Safari 18 on iPhone are built from the same source: see "Other browsers"). New since 0.3: a checklist for Chrome's own model (Gemini Nano) in the settings and on the website, which measures what it can and says where Chrome shows the rest; one row of the Activity list for each video, no counter on the toolbar icon, allow lists that win over a late answer and match a channel by handle or address, and "Allow channel" in the popup; the pages of the website at the foot of every page on a phone. It is a test build: see "What is tested" below. New in 0.3, since 0.2: the local model Kev-0.6B with a download button, a settings page with "Reset to defaults" everywhere, a break reminder that looks like a video or a comment, rules for the beliefs and ideologies the Church condemned (and for the books of the Index) that spare what warns against them, comments and recommended videos with the same options as the lists, an option to hide music videos, and a website that shows each feature after the filter has worked.

## The website

A Jekyll site built from the root of the repository (by `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`, with the builder of GitHub Pages) and served from Cloudflare Pages at https://vianney.cc: four pages (`index.html` Idea, `features.html`, `install.html`, `nano.html` Gemini Nano) and their data in `_data/`. On a phone the list of pages is one step back ("< Vianney", as in iOS Settings), so every page also carries the same list at its foot (`.foot-nav` in the layout). `node tools/make-site-nano.mjs` writes what the Nano page takes from the extension (copies of `nanocheck.js` and `nanoprobe.js` in `assets/nano/`, and `_data/nano.json`); a test checks that they are current. The longer texts in this repository (`plan.md`, `rules.md`, `sermons.md`) are not published.

- The Features page shows, for each feature, how it looks on YouTube once a filter has worked (never what it filtered: nothing flagged is on the site) and the piece of the settings page where it is set. The first is a picture, in light and dark: `python3 tools/make-feature-scenes.py` takes it from the real extension on the real YouTube (signed out, English), with our own titles, comments and gradients in place of the real content, so that no real person, channel or video is pictured; it needs the test browser of `tools/e2e-chrome.sh` and ImageMagick with WebP, and writes sizes and descriptions to `_data/shots.json`. The second is not a picture but HTML, so that its text keeps the size it has on the settings page at any width, can be searched and follows light and dark by itself: `python3 tools/make-feature-options.py` copies the markup of those groups from the real settings page (with the state it shows, without ids, popovers and handlers) into `_data/options.json`, and `node tools/make-options-css.mjs` writes `assets/options-view.css` from `options.css`, each rule under `.xopt`, for those blocks only. The options of a feature stand one under the other on the left; its first picture is on the right and a second one goes under the options, so that no room is left empty. The Rules feature shows one rule in its own format, from `_data/rules.json` (`node tools/make-site-data.mjs` writes it from the rule files, with the number of rules the site names).
- The install page links into the extension (Activity, Tweak settings). That needs a fixed extension ID: `manifest.json` carries a public `key`, the ID derived from it is in `_data/extension.yml` (`node tools/extension-id.mjs` prints it), and `web_accessible_resources` lets only `https://vianney.cc/*` and `https://www.vianney.cc/*` open the settings page. The private key was not kept. A browser does not let a web page open `chrome://` addresses, so the link to `chrome://extensions` copies the address. A store listing would get an ID of its own and the links would need it (not done).
- `?month=3` (also `mar`, `March`) shows the painting of another month. `lift_months` in `_data/wallpaper.yml` lists the months in which characters of the painting stand in front of the window by default (January, February, June, July, September, October), only on a tablet and a desktop, never on a phone; `?lift=overlap` shows them on any page of a wider screen. On a phone the site opens on the Idea page (the list of pages is one step back), and the painting of the month shows through, blurred.
- To look at the site on this computer, build it with Jekyll and serve the output; for the links into the extension to work there, the copy of the extension that is installed needs `http://127.0.0.1/*` in the `matches` of its `web_accessible_resources` entry (the real manifest names only the real site).

## Deploying the website

`.github/workflows/deploy.yml` runs for every push to `main` and by hand (never for a pull request: the repository is public). It runs `npm test`, builds the site with GitHub's Jekyll builder, uploads `_site` to the Cloudflare Pages project `vianney` with wrangler, and then `tools/cloudflare-pages.sh` makes sure that the project exists, that `vianney.cc` and `www.vianney.cc` are its custom domains with a proxied CNAME record each, and that the live site is the build that was just made (`tests/cloudflare-pages.test.js` runs that script against a stand-in for the API; `tests/workflow.test.js` checks what keeps the secrets safe).

- **Secrets** (Settings, Secrets and variables, Actions; or in the environment `production`, limited to the branch `main`): `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`.
- **The token** is a custom token with three permissions and nothing else: Account, Cloudflare Pages, Edit (on the account); Zone, DNS, Edit; Zone, Zone, Read (both on the zone `vianney.cc` only). The first is for the whole account, because Cloudflare cannot limit it to one Pages project: do not use a token that is also used for another site. The first step of the deploy reads Pages, the zone and its DNS records and names the permission that is missing before anything is uploaded (`DNS Settings` and `DNS Views` are for internal DNS: they are not the DNS permission). Optional variable `CLOUDFLARE_ZONE_ID`, if the token may not read zones.
- **A release** is made on this computer (`npm run release`, then commit `downloads/` and `_data/`); the workflow refuses a site whose download does not have the checksum that `downloads/latest.json` states.
- The script never deletes a DNS record: an A or AAAA record at one of the names stops it with a message, and a CNAME that does not point to a `pages.dev` address is left as it is.

## Try it

The short way: download the zip from the [install page](https://vianney.cc/install.html) and follow the steps there. From a clone of the repository:

1. Open `chrome://extensions`, switch on **Developer mode**, click **Load unpacked** and choose the `extension/` folder of this repository (or unzip the zip that `npm run pack` writes into a folder of its own and choose that folder; the files are at the top level of the zip, so Chrome also accepts the zip dropped onto the extensions page).
2. The settings page opens, laid out like System Settings: the rules in plain words with examples, a sensitivity slider per rule (how confident a rule must be before it acts) and a choice of what it does, each with a *Reset to defaults* button (every section has one at its end, and *Reset all settings* sits at the foot of the sidebar; the rules, the lists and everything ask first), and **Test rules**, where you can paste a title or some subtitles. **On-device AI** checks what your computer can run and has a *Download* button for Chrome's model.
3. Browse YouTube. Cards that match a rule are hidden, and **Activity** in the settings says why: one row for each video (or comment) however often it was looked at, with how many times, and buttons to allow or block the video or its channel. The toolbar icon carries no counter. What is checked has a switch each under **Scope**: lists and search results, the recommended videos next to a video, the video page itself, the comments under it, and the subtitles. A flagged comment is blurred, with a **Show** button; on a video page a flagged word in the subtitles is muted, and flagged words in the visible captions are masked.
   **Clean up** hides the extras around videos: Shorts, Games, Movies & TV, the Explore part of the sidebar, banners, music videos if you switch that on (they are the ones YouTube itself marks: a note in front of the length on the thumbnail, or the badge of an official artist channel; chant and hymns are music too, so it is off by default), and any shelf whose title you list (the list starts with "Von YouTube empfohlen" and "Recommended by YouTube").
   The toolbar icon opens a small panel in the manner of iOS: the switches that you change most, and the recent activity, without the full settings page.
4. The break reminder: set a short time in the settings (for example 1 minute) and play a video for that long. Then, at a random place and never first, the page gets one video among its videos (in a list, in the search results, or among the recommendations next to a video) or, under a video, one comment among the comments. It is drawn exactly like the real thing, with satirised clickbait for a title ("You wouldn't BELIEVE what happens if you take a BREAK NOW!") and, where the views would be, how long you have been watching. Nothing on it says what it is: only the portrait of St. Jean-Marie Vianney, as its picture and its channel icon, gives it away. It is no popup: ignore it, or use its three dots for "Remind me later" or "Dismiss". The settings have a switch for the videos and one for the comments.
5. Experimental, off by default: the **Images & Audio** section analyzes thumbnails and the audio of a playing video with Chrome's on-device model (it must offer image and audio input). Every part has a switch, every rule a slider, and allow and block lists win over the model.

Nothing leaves your browser, nothing learns from your viewing, and the extension needs no account. The optional on-device AI is off until you download it from the settings page; without it, the rules work from their word lists. There are two models, asked in turn: **Kev-0.6B**, which this extension downloads (346 MB from huggingface.co, 383 MB on a computer without WebGPU; Apache-2.0, English only; kept in the browser's private storage; checked against pinned SHA-256 values; Pause, Cancel and Delete are on the settings page), and Chrome's built-in model (Gemini Nano), which Chrome downloads. Kev answers first; when it is not sure, Chrome's model takes a second look. Either one alone works too. See "The local model" below.

## Other browsers

The code is the same for every browser; `tools/build.mjs` makes the three things that differ: the manifest, one small file (`extension/src/shared/target.js`: what the build offers) and, for Safari, the leaving out of the local model's files. Where a browser lacks a feature the code asks for the feature, not for the browser's name.

- **Firefox** (140 or newer; the manifest asks for it because AMO asks every new add-on to declare what data it collects: none). `node tools/build.mjs firefox` writes `dist/firefox/` and `dist/vianney-content-blocker-<version>-firefox.zip`. Try it: `about:debugging`, This Firefox, Load Temporary Add-on, choose `dist/firefox/manifest.json`. A normal Firefox installs only an add-on that Mozilla has signed: `tools/sign-firefox.sh` does it (unlisted: the file is signed, not published in the store) with the keys of an account at addons.mozilla.org in `WEB_EXT_API_KEY` and `WEB_EXT_API_SECRET`. Developer Edition, Nightly and ESR can switch the check off (`xpinstall.signatures.required`). The add-on ID is `vianney@vianney.cc` and cannot change once a signed version is out; if the signed file is to update itself from the site, an `update_url` has to be in the manifest before the first signing. Firefox has no offscreen documents, so the local model lives in the background page; it runs on WebAssembly on one thread (no WebGPU on Linux, and extension pages are not isolated enough for threads). `python3 -I tools/firefox-smoke.py` installs the build into a throwaway headless Firefox and checks it; with `--model` it also downloads the model and asks it a question.
- **Safari, for iPhone and iPad** (iOS 18 or newer). Apple's tool for the Xcode project runs only on a Mac: `tools/safari.sh` makes `dist/safari-xcode/` (open it in Xcode, choose your Team for the app and for its extension, run it on a device; a free Apple account runs it for seven days, TestFlight and the App Store need the paid program), and `tools/safari.sh --build` also builds it for the Simulator. Without a Mac, the workflow *Safari extension (iOS)* does the same on GitHub's and keeps the project for a week. Set `BUNDLE_ID` to an identifier that is yours. **Known limits:** the local model is off in this build (`kev: false`: it has not been tried on an iPhone, where a process that grows by hundreds of megabytes may be ended), Chrome's own model does not exist there, and Safari asks for each site once (Settings, Safari, Extensions). An iPhone opens `m.youtube.com`, a different page from the one the adapters were written for: the build matches it as well, **untested**; an iPad, or an iPhone that is asked for the desktop site, shows `www.youtube.com`, which is what everything here was made for. None of this has run on a device yet.

## Gemini Nano (Chrome's own model)

Chrome's built-in model is optional and, for a good reason, hard to get working, so the extension checks it itself. **Settings, On-device AI, Set up Gemini Nano** is a checklist (`core/nanocheck.js`, with the measurements in `shared/nanoprobe.js`): the browser and its version, the answer of the Prompt API and, when Chrome says "unavailable", the reason that Chrome gives (the error of `LanguageModel.create()`), the free disk space of the profile (an extension with `unlimitedStorage` has no quota of its own, so `storage.estimate()` is the free space; a web page's quota is a share of the whole disk and says nothing), the graphics chip, and for what no page can read (the "On-device AI" switch, the video memory, the policies) the page of Chrome and the line to read, each with a button that opens it. The website's page [Gemini Nano](https://vianney.cc/nano.html) says the same, runs the same checks in the page, and, when the extension is installed, asks it to open the pages of Chrome (a web page cannot: a link, `window.open` and `location` are all refused) and for the free disk space (`externally_connectable`, limited to the site and to a list of addresses).

What was found when this was written (Chromium 158 on Linux Mint, with what Chrome's documentation and source say where it could not be run):

- **No flag has to be changed in Chrome 154 and newer.** The flags of 158 are `#prompt-api` and `#prompt-api-multimodal-input` (both on by default); `#prompt-api-for-gemini-nano` and `#optimization-guide-on-device-model` are gone. The checklist lists the flags that exist for the version of Chrome it runs in.
- **Chromium is not Google Chrome.** The model is part of Google Chrome only: on Chromium 158 `create()` says "The feature flag gating model execution was disabled", also with the features forced on, and `chrome://on-device-internals` says "Enabled by user setting: false" because the switch "On-device AI" exists only in Chrome. To test the model, use Google Chrome.
- **Disk.** Chrome starts the download with 20 GiB free on the drive of the profile (the documentation says 22 GB) and keeps the model while 5 GiB is free (10 GB). Below that it does not start, and does not say so.
- **`chrome://on-device-internals` is hidden since Chrome 158** until `chrome://chrome-urls` has been used: press "Enable internal debugging pages". Then its "Broker State" lines say why, one by one.

## The local model

- **What it is.** Kev-0.6B (`onnx-community/kev-0.6b-ONNX`, 4-bit weights, base Qwen3-0.6B) answers the yes/no questions of the rules, one forward pass per question, with a probability. It runs in a hidden page of the extension (an offscreen document; Firefox and Safari have none, so the background page itself holds it: `kev-host.js`, chosen by `kev-backend.js` by asking whether `chrome.offscreen` exists), on the graphics chip through WebGPU when the browser has it (half precision when the chip offers `shader-f16`: 346 MB), else on the processor through WebAssembly (383 MB, about fifteen times slower: measured 1.8 s against 0.12 s per question on one computer).
- **Where the files are.** `extension/src/ai/`: `kev-manifest.js` (the pinned commit, sizes and SHA-256 of every file), `kev-core.js` (how a question is laid out for the model and how the answer is read), `kev-download.js` and `kev-download-worker.js` (the resumable downloader that checks the hashes while it writes), `kev-store.js` (the files in OPFS), `kev-offscreen.html` and `kev-offscreen.js` with `kev-runtime.js` (the model), `kev-backend.js` (the service worker's side), `ai-router.js` (which model is asked, in which order). `extension/vendor/` holds the runtime that reads the model (Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime Web with their licences and pinned hashes: `node tools/vendor-kev.mjs --check`). The weights are data and are not in the extension; the runtime is code and is, because an extension may not run code that it downloads. The extension zip is therefore about 10.7 MB, not 0.1 MB.
- **First use.** Settings, On-device AI, "Download Kev-0.6B". Chrome asks once for permission to talk to huggingface.co and hf.co; nothing else is requested and nothing is sent. The files go into the extension's private file system (OPFS), under a folder named for the pinned commit. A broken connection is resumed from the byte where it stopped; a file whose SHA-256 differs from the pin is deleted and reported. The model is loaded when the first question arrives (about two seconds on WebGPU), and unloaded again after five idle minutes (it holds 1 to 1.5 GB while loaded). Delete removes every file.
- **Check on the rule examples.** The same pane runs the example and spared titles of every rule through the real rules, with the word lists alone and with the models, and counts what the models change in both directions. The lines are the rule author's illustrations, not a test set. `research/kev-accuracy-smoke.md` has the same run in Node, with numbers.
- **Settings.** `ai.backend`: `auto` (Kev, then Chrome's model), `kev` or `prompt`. `ai.shots`: how many example lines go beside each question (core/shots.js).
- **Research and progress.** `research/kev-0.6b-browser.md` (the design basis), `research/kev-progress.md` (what was built and measured, gate by gate), `research/kev-accuracy-smoke.md`.

## What is tested

- `npm test`: unit tests for the text and word-list matching, the rule parser (and that the shipped rule files join into one clean pack), the engine (the deterministic stage, the model cascade, images and audio), sensitivity, allow and block lists, the audio-clip planner and WAV encoder, the break timer, the caption parser and the Prompt API backend (against a fake of the documented API). Every example and "left alone" title that a rule shows on the settings page is run through the real rules and word lists, so the page cannot promise what the rules do not do. The model check (what to download, timings) and the sources of the settings page and the website (icons, colours, type, the wallpaper, the length of the pages) are tested too. The website is also tested in a browser, on an emulated phone and on a desktop (`tools/e2e.py site`, against the built site). Needs Node 20 or newer, no dependencies.
- `tools/e2e-chrome.sh` then `python3 tools/e2e.py`: loads the extension into a throwaway headless Chrome and drives real YouTube pages (feed filtering, the slider, false alarms on religious searches and that a Catholic channel is spared, the break reminder as a video and as a comment (measured against the real neighbours), subtitle muting, the settings page and its model check, and the image and audio checks). It also searches from YouTube's own search box the way a person does, counts the frames in which a matching result is really painted (also with the processor slowed down), hovers over results to check the preview video, and takes pixel readings of screenshots. The test browser starts muted. For the audio check it needs `E2E_EXTRA_FLAGS="--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required"`.
- The local model: unit tests for the layout and decoding of questions (against a transcription of the model card's recipe and of the open-jev package), the SHA-256, the resumable downloader (against a real local server that cuts connections, flips a byte and answers 503), the layers and the status, and the counting of the check on the rule examples (`tests/kev-*.test.js`, `tests/modelcheck.test.js`). `python3 tools/kev-test-ext.py <folder>` makes a copy of the extension that may talk to `tools/kev-mirror.mjs`; with it, `KEV_FILES=<the pinned files> E2E_EXT_DIR=<folder> python3 tools/e2e.py kev` downloads the real files in a real Chrome, cuts the connection and resumes, refuses a flipped byte, asks a real YouTube title through the content script, the service worker and the offscreen document, and deletes everything.
- The image and audio checks are tested with a stand-in for Chrome's model that is put into the service worker: real thumbnails and real recorded audio go through the whole path, only the model's yes or no is scripted.
- Firefox: `tools/build.mjs` and its tests, the linter of Mozilla (`web-ext lint`: no errors), and `tools/firefox-smoke.py` in a real Firefox 161: the add-on installs, the content script starts, the settings page and the background page answer, and with `--model` the local model is downloaded (383 MB, checked against its hashes) and answers questions on WebAssembly. Safari: the manifest, the script and the workflow are tested as text; nothing has run on a Mac or an iPhone.
- **Not verified yet:** subtitles from a real signed session (an anonymous headless browser gets an empty caption body from YouTube, so this needs a normal profile), the Prompt API with a real model in Google Chrome (the checklist was run against Chromium, which has none), the Safari build on a device, YouTube itself in Firefox and Safari, and any accuracy or speed figure for the image and audio checks (this needs the model downloaded, and the test machine cannot hold it).
