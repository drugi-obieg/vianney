# The Curé d'Ars on guarding the eyes and the ears

This project takes its name and its starting idea from Jean-Marie Vianney (1786–1859), the Curé of Ars and patron saint of parish priests. This page collects what he wrote on the subject, with sources, so that the rules the extension ships with can be traced back to his text and not to a paraphrase.

**Where it is.** The passage is in his Ash Wednesday sermon *On Penance* (*Sur la Pénitence*). Among the outward mortifications he lists the senses one by one, and the eyes and the ears come first. Four other sermons add to it; they are below. The passages were found by searching all 85 sermons of the four-volume edition for "yeux" and "oreilles" close together and reading each hit in context. Other editions and the catechism instructions were not searched, so if a better-fitting sermon exists, please [open an issue](https://github.com/drugi-obieg/vianney/issues).

## 1. The passage: *Sur la Pénitence*

<figure class="quote">
<blockquote lang="fr">Pour la mortification extérieure, elle consiste à mortifier notre corps avec tous ses sens : 1° Nous devons mortifier nos yeux : ne rien regarder par curiosité, ni différents objets qui pourraient nous porter à avoir quelques mauvaises pensées ; ne point lire de livres qui ne sont pas capables de nous porter à la vertu, qui, au contraire, ne peuvent que nous en détourner et éteindre le peu de foi que nous avons. 2° Nous devons mortifier nos oreilles : ne point écouter avec plaisir toutes ces chansons, ces discours qui peuvent nous flatter et qui n'aboutissent à rien : c'est toujours un temps bien mal employé et ravi aux soins que nous devons donner à notre âme ; ne jamais prendre plaisir à écouter les médisances et les calomnies.</blockquote>
<blockquote class="tr" lang="en">As for outward mortification, it consists in mortifying our body with all its senses. First, we must mortify our eyes: look at nothing out of curiosity, nor at the various things that could lead us to evil thoughts; read no books that are not able to lead us to virtue, books that on the contrary can only turn us away from it and extinguish the little faith we have. Second, we must mortify our ears: not listen with pleasure to all those songs and speeches that may flatter us and lead to nothing; it is always time badly spent and taken from the care we owe to our soul; never take pleasure in listening to detraction and slander.</blockquote>
<figcaption>St. Jean-Marie Vianney, <em>Sur la Pénitence</em> (Ash Wednesday), part III, "mortification extérieure". Read in context: <a href="https://livres-mystiques.com/partieTEXTES/Ars/Sermons/tome1/penite15.html">livres-mystiques.com</a> or <a href="https://www.apotre.org/livres/vianney-sermons/12">apotre.org, page 12</a>.</figcaption>
</figure>

The sentence that follows is the one that fits a feed best:

<figure class="quote">
<blockquote lang="fr">Oui, M. F., nous devons nous mortifier en tout cela et ne pas être du nombre de ces personnes curieuses qui veulent savoir tout ce que l'on a dit, ce que l'on a fait, d'où l'on vient, ce que l'on veut, ce que l'on nous a dit.</blockquote>
<blockquote class="tr" lang="en">Yes, my brethren, we must mortify ourselves in all of this, and not be among those curious people who want to know everything that has been said, what has been done, where one comes from, what one wants, what was said to us.</blockquote>
<figcaption>Same sermon, same passage.</figcaption>
</figure>

What to take from it:

- **Curiosity comes first.** The eyes are guarded against looking "par curiosité" before anything else. A feed that runs on curiosity (thumbnails, titles written to be clicked) is the target.
- **Reading and viewing are named together.** The same rule covers books and, by extension, what we choose to watch.
- **The ears have two separate problems.** Idle talk and songs "that lead to nothing" are lost time. Taking pleasure in detraction and slander is named on its own.

## 2. Closing the eyes is not enough: *Sur le Monde*

The sermon for the 14th Sunday after Pentecost retells a story it attributes to St. Augustine (*Confessions*, Book VI). A young man, Alypius, is dragged to the amphitheatre against his will and resolves to keep his eyes shut.

<figure class="quote">
<blockquote lang="fr">Mais Alype eut beau dire, ils l'emmenèrent, et, pendant que tout l'amphithéâtre était dans les transports de ces barbares plaisirs, le jeune homme défendait à son cœur d'y prendre part, et à ses yeux de regarder, en les tenant fermés. Ah ! plût à Dieu qu'il se fût bouché aussi les oreilles ; car, ayant été frappé d'un grand cri qui se fit entendre, la curiosité l'emporta : ne voulant voir que cela, il ouvrit les yeux, c'en fut assez pour le perdre.</blockquote>
<blockquote class="tr" lang="en">But Alypius protested in vain; they took him along, and while the whole amphitheatre was in transports of these barbarous pleasures, the young man forbade his heart to take part and his eyes to look, keeping them shut. Ah, would to God he had stopped his ears as well! For, struck by a great cry that was heard, curiosity won out: wanting to see just that, he opened his eyes, and that was enough to ruin him.</blockquote>
<figcaption>St. Jean-Marie Vianney, <em>Sur le Monde</em> (14th Sunday after Pentecost). Read in context: <a href="https://www.apotre.org/livres/vianney-sermons/35">apotre.org, page 35</a>.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Our reading, not the Curé's words:** a guarded eye does not help if the ear stays open, because the sound arrives before the choice. That is the reason the extension cannot stop at thumbnails. Once a video plays, its audio is the harder door, and the plan treats subtitles as the way to reach it.

## 3. If nobody listened: *Sur la Médisance*

<figure class="quote">
<blockquote lang="fr">Je finis en disant que, non seulement, il est mal fait de médire et de calomnier, mais encore d'écouter la médisance et la calomnie avec plaisir ; car si personne n'écoutait, il n'y aurait pas de médisants. Par là, on se rend complice de tout le mal que fait le médisant. Saint Bernard nous dit qu'il est très difficile de savoir qui est le plus coupable de celui qui médit ou de celui qui écoute ; l'un a le démon sur la langue et l'autre dans les oreilles.</blockquote>
<blockquote class="tr" lang="en">I close by saying that it is not only wrong to detract and to slander, but also to listen to detraction and slander with pleasure; for if no one listened, there would be no detractors. By this one makes oneself an accomplice in all the harm the detractor does. St. Bernard tells us it is very hard to know which is more guilty, the one who speaks ill or the one who listens; the one has the devil on his tongue, the other in his ears.</blockquote>
<figcaption>St. Jean-Marie Vianney, <em>Sur la Médisance</em> (11th Sunday after Pentecost). Read in context: <a href="https://www.livres-mystiques.com/partieTEXTES/Ars/Sermons/tome2/medisance21.html">livres-mystiques.com</a>.</figcaption>
</figure>

This is the textual basis for the **gossip rule**. The viewer who keeps watching a video about a named person's private faults is the listener in this passage.

## 4. The Lord's name: *Sur le deuxième Commandement*

<figure class="quote">
<blockquote lang="fr">Il est bien étonnant, M.F., que le bon Dieu soit obligé de nous faire un commandement pour nous défendre de profaner son saint nom.</blockquote>
<blockquote class="tr" lang="en">It is astonishing, my brethren, that the good God should be obliged to give us a commandment to forbid us to profane his holy name.</blockquote>
<figcaption>St. Jean-Marie Vianney, <em>Sur le deuxième Commandement de Dieu</em> (5th Sunday after Pentecost). Read in context: <a href="https://www.livres-mystiques.com/partieTEXTES/Ars/Sermons/tome2/commande12.html">livres-mystiques.com</a>.</figcaption>
</figure>

The sermon is also a taxonomy. He says he will explain "la différence qu'il y a entre les jurements, les blasphèmes, les reniements, les imprécations, les malédictions et les paroles grossières" (the difference between oaths, blasphemies, renunciations, imprecations, curses and coarse words), and complains that people confuse them. His own examples, quoted exactly:

- **Oath** (*jurement*): "prendre le bon Dieu à témoin de ce que l'on dit ou promet", taking God as witness of what one says or promises.
- **Blasphemy**: "Le bon Dieu n'est pas juste de me faire souffrir ou perdre cela." He says this is a blasphemy and not an oath.
- **Imprecation**: "Si j'ai fait cela, je ne veux jamais voir la face de Dieu !"
- **Insults and obscenities** are separate again: calling a neighbour a thief is "lui dire des injures", and dirty talk is "des obscénités".
- **Using the name in vain** is, citing St. Thomas, "un grand péché", and unlike other sins it is not made lighter by a light matter. Of the oath he says "plus la matière est légère, plus la profanation est grande" (the lighter the matter, the greater the profanation).

**Design note.** The extension's word-and-context rules for the Lord's name should start from this list rather than from a generic profanity list. The last point is about oaths sworn on trifles. Whether a casual exclamation falls under it is a judgment for the person writing the rules, and the software should not decide it silently.

## 5. Eyes and ears once more: *Sur la pureté*

<figure class="quote">
<blockquote lang="fr">Si nous en apercevons quelqu'une mal arrangée, il faut bien vite nous en détourner, et non pas faire comme ceux qui ont des yeux impudiques, qui s'y arrêtent autant que le démon le veut. Il faut mortifier nos oreilles, ne jamais prendre plaisir à entendre des paroles ou chansons sales.</blockquote>
<blockquote class="tr" lang="en">If we catch sight of someone improperly dressed, we must turn away from it quickly, and not do as those who have shameless eyes, which linger as long as the devil wishes. We must mortify our ears, never taking pleasure in hearing filthy words or songs.</blockquote>
<figcaption>St. Jean-Marie Vianney, <em>Sur la pureté</em> (17th Sunday after Pentecost), closing paragraph. Read in context: <a href="https://www.apotre.org/livres/vianney-sermons/38">apotre.org, page 38</a>.</figcaption>
</figure>

The verb is "se détourner", to turn away, and it is meant to be quick. That is what the **hide** action is for: removing the thing before the eye has lingered on it.

## 6. Doubtful as doubtful: *Sur les qualités de la confession*

<figure class="quote">
<blockquote lang="fr">Je dis qu'il faut qu'elle soit simple ; c'est-à-dire éviter toutes ces accusations inutiles, tous ces scrupules qui font dire cent fois la même chose, qui font perdre le temps au confesseur, fatiguent ceux qui attendent pour se confesser, et éteignent la dévotion. Il faut se montrer tel que l'on est par une déclaration sincère ; il faut accuser ce qui est douteux comme douteux, ce qui est certain comme certain.</blockquote>
<blockquote class="tr" lang="en">I say that it must be simple; that is, avoid all those useless accusations, all those scruples that make one say the same thing a hundred times, that waste the confessor's time, tire those waiting to confess, and extinguish devotion. One must show oneself as one is, by a sincere declaration; one must accuse what is doubtful as doubtful, and what is certain as certain.</blockquote>
<figcaption>St. Jean-Marie Vianney, <em>Sur les qualités de la confession</em> (unpublished sermons, volume 4). Read in context: <a href="https://www.livres-mystiques.com/partieTEXTES/Ars/Sermons/tome4/18confession.htm">livres-mystiques.com</a>.</figcaption>
</figure>

This is about confession, not software, and is quoted only as the source of a design principle. A filter should say "certain" when the model is certain and "doubtful" when it is not, and it should not fill the day with warnings about borderline cases. The plan's per-rule sensitivity control, which lets the user decide how confident a rule must be before it flags, and its calm wording follow from this.

## 7. What the extension takes from this

- **Eyes:** feed cards, search results and "up next" are judged before they are shown, and the default action is to hide.
- **Ears:** once a video plays, its subtitles are checked, and offending words or passages can be muted or skipped.
- **Gossip:** a separate rule, because the sermon treats the listener as an accomplice.
- **The Lord's name:** a taxonomy taken from the sermon, with context-sensitive rules, because the same words can be a prayer or a curse.
- **Honest doubt:** every verdict carries a probability, and uncertain cases are shown as uncertain.

See the [plan](plan.md) for how this becomes software.

## 8. Sources and notes

- **Edition.** *Sermons du Saint Curé d'Ars* (Lyon). The first edition was approved on 20 August 1882, and the text read here carries the archbishop's approval of a new edition dated 26 September 1893. The French is public domain.
- **Transcriptions.** Volumes 1, 2 and 4 are quoted from livres-mystiques.com. In volume 3 of that site the accented letters were already lost in the source files, so the passages from volume 3 (*Sur le Monde*, *Sur la pureté*) are quoted from apotre.org, whose text states that it comes from jesusmarie.free.fr. Line-break hyphens in that copy were removed; nothing else was changed.
- **Translations.** The English text is a working, literal translation made for this project. It is not an official or published translation, and the French is the authoritative wording.
- **Limits.** The passages were found by word search. This page does not claim that they are the only places where the Curé treats these subjects.
