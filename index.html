Vianney Content Blocker

A YouTube filter that runs entirely in your browser. You write the rules in plain text, a small local model applies them, and what you decided against never reaches your eyes or ears.

Read the plan The sermons behind it

Why it exists

YouTube's recommendation engine picks what you see next in order to keep you watching. This project moves that decision back to you: rules you wrote yourself, applied before the page is drawn.

The idea comes from the Curé of Ars, St. Jean-Marie Vianney, who in a sermon for Ash Wednesday lists the senses one by one, and puts the eyes and the ears first:

What it will do

  • Hide, not just warn

    Feed cards, search results, "up next" and Shorts can be hidden, blanked, or refilled until the page has enough items that pass your rules.

  • Rules in plain text

    You write questions about the content (yes or no, pick one, score it) and say what to do about it. No code, no account.

  • A slider for every rule

    Each rule has a control for how confident it must be before it flags. If one flags too much or too little, you move it, and the settings show what that would have changed.

  • Eyes first, then ears

    Titles, descriptions and channels are judged before you click. Once a video plays, its subtitles are checked, and a word or a passage can be muted or skipped.

  • A break, in the feed

    After the number of hours you choose, a card that looks like a recommendation tells you that you have been watching for a while and should take a break.

  • Local and repeatable

    Models run on your machine. No telemetry and no learning from your viewing: the same input and the same rules give the same answer.

A rule, as currently planned:

rule gossip
  where: watch
  ask score: "How much of this talks about the private faults of a named person?"
    levels: none, mild, heavy
  flag at: heavy
  action: warn-overlay

Where things stand

Status

  • Research is written up in the plan: which small models can answer custom questions inside a browser tab, what YouTube lets an extension read (measured, not assumed), and where the open risks are.
  • First draft target: Chrome's built-in model behind a deterministic pre-filter, with a small local English model as a second opinion, chosen by measurement on a labelled set.
  • The sermon research is done and sourced: the sermons page.
  • A first testable build (version 0.1) is in the repository: feed filtering, subtitle muting, a slider per rule and the break reminder, checked end to end in a real browser. It loads unpacked, so it is for testing, not yet for everyday use.

This is a personal project and will be distributed from its own website first, not through a browser store. The source and the issue tracker are on GitHub.
